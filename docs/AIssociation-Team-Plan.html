Agreed product & scope

This document is the team’s implementation handoff for the 18 November midterm. It defines the product baseline, named responsibilities, acceptance criteria and integration checkpoints. Product requirements are settled for this delivery; proposed technical defaults and unresolved edge cases are listed separately.

Agreed requirementExecution proposalChoice still openTeacher obligation

The public platform

One AIssociation account lets a student discover public events across associations and register as a participant. Public browsing does not require an account; registration does.

Combined event discovery, a calendar, event details, and each association’s public presentation page are included.

Association workspaces

Membership is separate from having an account. Each association owns its internal administration, projects, recruitment, reporting hierarchy, tasks, groups, and messages.

One user can belong to several associations with different permissions. All active members can enter their association’s backoffice.

Midterm: 18 November 2026, 16:00 — Europe/Bucharest.
Submission channel and whether a live seminar demonstration is required still need confirmation. Prepare a runnable demonstration either way.

Included: accounts and membership; project teams and recruitment; hierarchical tasks; events and public discovery/calendar; participant registration; manual attendance; association/project/department chats, custom groups, and direct messages.

Later: QR attendance, direct Google/WhatsApp integrations, custom domains/site builder, attachments, push notifications, and a general workflow/permission editor. Chat transport and advanced chat behaviors are not yet agreed; periodic refresh and plain text are proposed below.

Implementation baseline: the monorepo, local Docker setup, hosting and CORS configuration are established. Domain workflows remain to be implemented. Each owner must inspect the current main branch before starting and verify their workflow on the integrated application. Coordinate daily and raise blocked dependencies immediately.

04 / EXECUTION PLAN

Permission rules & examples

Rank alone is insufficient. Access depends on the current association, project, and reporting branch. One user’s management position in project A grants no corresponding access in project B.

ActorTasks they can readActions
Association administratorAll tasks in their association.Create, assign, reassign, and update tasks within that association.
Project managerOwn tasks and subordinate tasks in their project branch. Peer managers’ own tasks and unrelated branches remain hidden.Create, assign, reassign, and update tasks for subordinates.
Department managerOwn tasks and tasks below them in their project department/team branch.Create, assign, reassign, and update subordinate tasks.
Project memberOwn tasks only.Update own task status; not assignment, deadline, or task description.
Unrelated association member / participantNo internal task access.No internal task operations.

Examples to test

  • Two department managers cannot see each other’s tasks or the other department’s subordinate tasks.
  • A project manager sees their department managers’ and those managers’ subordinates’ tasks.
  • Two project managers at the same level cannot inspect each other’s own tasks or unrelated reporting branches.
  • A member assigned to a different project cannot obtain access by changing the project/task identifier.
  • Association-administrator access overrides task-branch restrictions only inside their own association.
  • Shared department chat does not grant visibility into colleagues’ tasks.

Project overview: ordinary members may see project summaries; detailed project spaces are available to the assigned team and association administrators. Exact public-summary fields should be agreed in the first contract session.

Reporting invariant: no cycles or cross-project supervisors. One accepted position/reporting manager per member per project. Top-level project managers have no manager inside that project; department managers report to the appointing project manager. This is the proposed concrete representation of the agreed hierarchy.

Gabriel · Accounts, workspaces & administration

Primary workflow: participant account → association membership → authorized workspace access.

What to deliver

  1. Implement participant registration/sign-in/sign-out using the agreed authentication approach; provide the current-user/membership contract.
  2. Prepare two demonstration associations and initial administrators; provide administration to add existing users and assign association membership roles.
  3. Implement workspace selection and the common association/authorization infrastructure. Distinguish public participants from association members.
  4. Integrate permission conventions with Raul/Tudor, verify Render/Vercel/database configuration, and maintain environment/deployment instructions.
  5. Conduct short interviews, assemble the research summary, and coordinate the requirements/traceability and submission package.

Acceptance checklist

  • An account registers for public events without becoming an association member.
  • One account can enter two memberships and have different rights in each.
  • A non-member cannot read internal resources or messages; a member cannot promote themselves.
  • A second developer can run the application using the documented setup.

Additional artifact: Architecture/deployment runbook; requirements/research coordination. The research form is reserved as Gabriel’s proposed independently written feature.

Raul · Projects, recruitment & hierarchy

Primary workflow: project creation → recruitment applications → selection → team assignment.

What to deliver

  1. Implement administrator-created projects and project-manager appointments; implement manager editing of project details and recruitment positions.
  2. Implement recruitment call/deadline, position selection and motivation, multiple position applications, and manager accept/reject actions.
  3. Make acceptance atomic: validate eligibility and reporting assignment, create project membership, and enforce one accepted position per member/project.
  4. Implement project-manager appointment of department managers and assignment of members within the correct reporting structure.
  5. Own the initial ERD and coordinate ordered database migrations with all endpoint owners. Publish project/team APIs early.

Acceptance checklist

  • Only current members of the association can apply.
  • A member can submit distinct position applications, but cannot obtain two accepted positions in the same project.
  • Accepted members enter the correct project/team branch; an unrelated manager cannot process the application.
  • Hierarchy rejects cycles and cross-project/cross-association assignments.

Additional artifact: ERD, entity relationships, migration index, and recruitment/team decision notes.

Handoffs: Gabriel supplies accounts/memberships. Tudor needs the reporting-tree contract. Yasmine needs project/event ownership. Tudor and Denisa need chat audience membership.

Tudor · Tasks, authorization & messaging API

Primary workflow: manager assigns task → member updates → permitted manager monitors/reassigns.

What to deliver

  1. Implement task creation, assignment, mandatory deadline, status updates, and manager reassignment.
  2. Implement reporting-branch task queries and write checks; prevent peer or unrelated-branch exposure.
  3. Provide association/project/department/custom-group/direct-message APIs and persistent text messages with sender/time.
  4. Enforce association and conversation membership on every list/send operation. Derive project/department audiences from the team model.
  5. Publish the messaging API with Denisa and maintain the shared API/error and authorization-test matrix.

Acceptance checklist

  • Own/subordinate tasks are visible; peers and unrelated branches are denied.
  • A member cannot alter another person’s task or their own assignment/deadline.
  • Managers can update/reassign only within their allowed branch; association administrators can operate association-wide.
  • A non-member cannot list/send group messages; a direct conversation cannot cross associations.

Additional artifact: API contract and authorization test matrix; messaging schema/API in coordination with Raul.

Handoffs: Gabriel supplies identity/association checks; Raul supplies project memberships/reporting. Agree messaging request/response formats with Denisa before either side builds independently.

Yasmine · Events, public pages, discovery & calendar

Primary workflow: organizer creates/publishes event → visitor discovers it through association/platform views.

What to deliver

  1. Implement event draft editing/publication with the required descriptive/date/location fields, mandatory registration deadline, and optional capacity.
  2. Support project-linked events and administrator-managed standalone events. Authorized publication requires no approval queue.
  3. Build association public profiles with their published events and the platform homepage/listing.
  4. Implement shared association/date filtering and a simple calendar backed by the same published-event data.
  5. Own wireframes and short usability checks; implement the backend event/query slice with Gabriel pairing where needed.

Acceptance checklist

  • A project can contain several events; an event can exist without a project.
  • Draft events stay private. A published event appears on both the association page and platform listing/calendar.
  • Unauthorized organizers cannot edit another project/association’s event.
  • The registration deadline is required and can be extended by an authorized organizer; Denisa’s flow sees the updated value.

Additional artifact: Wireframes, main screen flow map, and usability findings/change notes.

Handoffs: Gabriel supplies auth/public-profile administration conventions; Raul supplies project access. Publish event fields and public detail responses early for Denisa.

Denisa · Participation, attendance & messaging UI

Primary workflow: discover event → register/cancel → organizer views roster and records attendance.

What to deliver

  1. Implement participant registration/cancellation APIs and screens with signed-in identity, optional capacity and mandatory deadline checks.
  2. Implement organizer roster, authorized cancellation after deadline, and manual attendance/correction.
  3. Implement messaging UI for association/project/department chats, direct conversations, and creating custom groups.
  4. Use one shared conversation/message interface across chat types; integrate with Tudor’s API and handle loading/empty/error states.
  5. Own workflow test scenarios and the demonstration script. Implement the registration/attendance backend slice with pairing support.

Acceptance checklist

  • Registration confirms immediately; duplicate active registration is prevented.
  • Two attempts for the last seat cannot overbook. A missing capacity means unlimited registration.
  • Participant cancellation before cutoff releases capacity; after cutoff the participant is denied while an authorized organizer may cancel.
  • Roster/attendance access is scoped to the event’s organizers; repeated attendance updates do not duplicate records.
  • Chat navigation and group creation never expose members/messages from another association.

Additional artifact: End-to-end workflow test scenarios and demo script; messaging UI integration evidence.

Definition of done & demonstration

Workflow completion criteria

  1. Its agreed behavior and permission rules are written and linked to requirement IDs.
  2. UI, API, business logic, and PostgreSQL persistence work together.
  3. Validation, loading/empty/error states, and unauthorized access are handled.
  4. Important positive and negative tests pass; screenshots alone are not proof of business-rule correctness.
  5. A reviewed PR is merged; hosted behavior is checked and instructions are current.
  6. The owner can explain the implementation, tradeoffs, tests, and their actual contribution.

Integrated demonstration scenario

  1. Show two distinct associations and a student with a participant account.
  2. Administrator adds membership; the member enters only permitted workspaces.
  3. Administrator creates a project and appoints its manager. Manager sets recruitment positions and department managers.
  4. Member applies to multiple positions; manager accepts one and assigns the reporting manager.
  5. Manager assigns a task; member changes status. Demonstrate denied peer and unrelated-branch access.
  6. Publish multiple events under one project and a standalone event; discover them on association pages, combined listing and calendar.
  7. Participant registers immediately; show a capacity/deadline rejection and permitted cancellation.
  8. Organizer views roster, marks attendance and corrects it.
  9. Exchange messages in appropriate association/project/department spaces; demonstrate a custom group and a direct message, with no cross-association access.

Use synthetic demo data and separate browser sessions/accounts where useful. Keep a clean seed/reset procedure and a fallback local demonstration using Docker.
